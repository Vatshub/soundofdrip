        How the scan works

        SongScan measures the sound itself. It doesn’t look the song up online, and it doesn’t guess from the title.

        Tempo

        It finds the moments where new sounds start, like drum hits and plucked notes, then looks for the gap that repeats most often. That gap becomes the beats per minute.

        Key

        It adds up how much of each of the 12 notes you hear across the whole song, then compares that pattern with how major and minor keys usually sound.

        Energy

        It tracks loudness every quarter of a second, smooths it out, and marks each stretch as quiet, medium or loud against the song’s own peak.

        Chords

        It lines up a grid of beats and bars, checks which notes sound in every half bar, and picks the closest major or minor chord, favouring the note in the bass.

        Sections

        It marks a new section where the chords or loudness clearly change, groups sections that sound alike, then names them: the loudest repeated part is the likely chorus.

        What to keep in mind

        • It works best on songs with a steady beat. Live recordings and free-tempo pieces can give a rough number.
        • Tempo can come out at half or double what you feel, which is common in drum and bass and trap. SongScan shows both.
        • A key can be confused with its partner key, like A minor and C major, because they share the same notes. When it’s close, SongScan tells you the runner-up.
        • Chords are simplified to major and minor, so a sevenths or suspended chord shows up as its closest plain chord. Busy mixes can blur them.
        • Section names are educated guesses. A song with one chorus, or a quiet chorus, can be labelled differently than you’d expect.
        • It doesn’t detect genre yet.
